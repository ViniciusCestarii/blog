## What to draw

Your call, but read this part carefully, because there is one specific way to
get it wrong.

**Go down, not up.** The wrong move is to ask what the post is *about* and
paint the answer. Every post on this blog is about protocols, communication,
and processes talking to each other, so that question always returns the same
answer and the covers all collapse into the same picture. Do not paint the
theme. Paint one small concrete oddity from *this* post and nothing else: a
thread that goes to sleep while another does its work, a single byte written
for no purpose except to wake something up, two numbers packed into eight
bytes, a request that will never be answered because nobody is listening, a
thing kept after it was rejected. Find that detail in the post you were given,
then find an image for it. If your idea would fit any other post on this blog,
it is the wrong idea and you should go back and pick something smaller.

**Vary the register.** These covers sit next to each other on one site and must
not look like a set of variations. Before you settle, consider which of these
the post actually calls for, and do not default to the first: a single object
alone, lit and studied; an interior with nobody in it; a figure alone, doing
nothing; a crowd, or many of one thing; architecture, a threshold, a stairway,
a passage; a landscape with the subject small inside it; a close study of one
hand, one fold of cloth, one surface. Some posts want a person. Many do not.

Renaissance painters were fluent in the underlying problem, which is how to
paint something that has no body. They solved it with light, with what they
left out of the frame, and with a moment chosen just before or just after the
one you expect. Do the same.

Figures are welcome and need not be human. Angels, demons, something older and
larger that the painting only shows part of, or no figure at all. If you put
something inhuman in the frame, reveal less of it than you want to: a portion at
the edge, a shape the light does not fully reach. The moment it is fully
described it stops being a painting and becomes an illustration.

If a face appears, the expression stays unreadable. It does not tell you what
it has decided.

Already used on this blog, so do not draw it again: two figures with something
passing between them, one offering and one receiving, a messenger arriving, a
handshake, an object held out in an open palm, two of anything facing each
other in balance. This is the obvious image for every post here, which is
exactly why it is now off the table.

Do not draw: circuit boards, padlocks, shields, hooded figures, glowing
networks, coins, chains, matrix rain, floating cubes, gears as a stand-in for
"systems", keys, eyes as a symbol, lighthouses, hands reaching toward each other
in the Sistine pose, halos, glowing auras, horned red devils, skulls, hourglasses.

Leave out anything that would normally carry writing: scrolls, books, open
codices, banners, tablets, cartouches, signs, labels, maps, clock faces,
armillary rings, astrolabes.

## Style: fixed

A Renaissance painting: oil on panel, Northern Italian, early sixteenth
century, in the manner of Leonardo. Seen now, not when it was made. The varnish
has darkened, the surface has aged, and it has been photographed in a dim room
rather than reproduced in a catalogue.

**Palette, stay inside it:**
- ground and deep shadow, `#111111` warming toward `#2a2a2e`
- the mass of the painting in olive-grey mid-tones, `#595856`
- half-light on form, `#afafaf`
- flesh and the brightest cloth in warm bone-cream, `#d8d6d3` to `#f8f7f5`,
  never pure white
- one earth-red note, `#b8410f`, lifting to `#ff8a4c` where the light strikes
  it, on the single element that matters most, usually a drapery fold or a
  sleeve

Most of the panel is dark. The light arrives from one side and finds only what
matters.

**Painting and finish:**
- Sfumato: edges dissolving softly into shadow, no hard outline anywhere on
  flesh, transitions built in thin layers rather than drawn.
- Strong chiaroscuro. Large areas of the panel carry no information at all,
  and the eye is led by light alone.
- Modelling that respects real anatomy and real drapery weight. Cloth falls,
  it does not float.
- Visible age: fine craquelure across the surface, darkened varnish warming
  the whole image, slight unevenness in the panel, a little grime in the
  deepest passages.
- Photographed rather than scanned: a faint sheen where the light crosses the
  varnish, soft falloff toward the panel edges.

**Composition and mood:**
- Three-quarter view, close but not intimate. Shallow depth, dark ground
  behind. If there is a landscape, it is small, distant, and hazy.
- Held stillness. The moment before an answer, not the answer.
- A painting from the middle of a story whose beginning is not shown.

**Never:** neon, digital glow, lens flare, 3D renders, airbrush gradients,
anime, flat vector illustration, photorealism, modern devices or screens,
fantasy-illustration or trading-card rendering, symmetrical occult diagrams,
gold-leaf icon style, anything that reads as a fantasy book cover rather than
as a museum panel.
